# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added (lecturers)
- **Breaks.** *Break* in the bar of a live lecture pauses the whole lecture (a coffee break, lunch): listening stops, students see "on a break" and cannot answer or flag, the lecture's minutes stop counting, and *Resume* brings it back, switching listening on again if it was on. The lecture record counts teaching time only and shows each break (`[BREAK …]`). Migration `027`; routes `POST /api/live/sessions/:id/pause` and `/resume`.
- **Chapters, like laps on a stopwatch.** *Chapter* in the bar (and in the island) starts a new chapter at that moment; names can be added now or after the lecture, chapters can be joined, and they replace the automatic parts in the review, the notes and the downloads as soon as there is more than one. Migration `027`; `src/modules/live/lecture-edits.js`.
- **Delete what was recorded.** A lecturer can delete the recording of one chapter (transcript lines, audio, and the key points and excerpts from that stretch), only the audio, or everything. Students cannot, and neither can another lecturer.
- **Ready-made lecture times.** Scheduling a lecture offers the three sessions of the teaching day, taken from the printed DTM timetable: morning 08:15–11:30 (periods 1–2), afternoon 14:00–15:30 (period 4), evening 15:45–19:00 (periods 5–6), and the single periods. One tap sets the start and the length. The date picker's quick times are the period starts. `tests/lecture-slots.test.js` checks them against the timetable data.
- **Two more student flags: "Explanation too long" and "A bit boring".** They show in the lecturer's flag counts, the chart, the review and the downloads like the other flags, but they are feedback on the teaching: they do not mark a part for follow-up and are never something a student has to study. Migration `028` rebuilds `live_flags` with the wider list and keeps every stored flag.

### Changed
- **The island is a circle first.** It starts as a small frosted circle instead of a toolbar. A tap opens the tools; the person drags it anywhere, turns the bar across or down, fades the circle until it is barely visible (it comes back at a touch or when the pointer is near), or hides it again. Place, direction and fade are remembered on that device. The bar opens towards the side with room and never leaves the screen.

### Fixed
- **The lecture notes viewer showed only a blur.** The hidden "All notes" and chat layers set their own display, which beat the hidden attribute, so a frosted full-screen sheet sat on top of every slide. They are really hidden now.
- **Quick actions and the mobile tab bar were not centred.** On a phone a two-word label such as "Join lecture" or "My progress" wrapped and hugged the left edge of its column; labels are centred now and wrap evenly.

### Renamed
- **CampusOS is gone from everything people see or download**: the headers of exported text files, download file names, the timetable data file and its header, the service worker cache, the Android user agent and wake-lock names, the package name, the scripts, the server templates (`deploy/citrus*.service`) and the guides. Kept on purpose because renaming them would break something already installed or saved: the Android application id, the database file `storage/campusos.db`, the device protocol name `campusos-device/1`, the calendar domain `campusos.local` and the default admin address `admin@campusos.local`.

### Renamed (earlier)
- **Campus OS and its assistant are now called Citrus.** The name changed everywhere people see it: the app, the sign-in and sign-up pages, notifications, the German translations, what the assistant says ("Ask Citrus", "Hey Citrus"), the Android app (name, texts and notification) and the documentation. The mark is a **C** now (the old ring, opened on the right with round ends): in the wordmark (a gradient C followed by "itrus"), on the sign-in cards, as the favicon, in the home-screen and notification icons and in the Android launcher icon. Saying "Hey Citrus" wakes the assistant ("Sitrus" and the German "Zitrus" are understood too, fruit talk such as "citrus juice" is not taken for a call, and the old name still works); a person who gave the assistant another name in the settings keeps it, and the interface keeps saying Citrus where it means the product ("Open Citrus", "your Citrus password"). "Campus" stays where it means the campus itself (Campus life, Campus map, Campus feed, Weidener Technologie-Campus). Kept as they were, because renaming them would break existing installs and links: folder, repository and file names, the Android package id (`app.campusos.android`), the database file name, service names and the `CampusOSClient` code library. The address is **https://citrus-mdu.vercel.app** now (the Android app opens it); the old address, campusos-mdu.vercel.app, keeps working for links and QR codes already out there. The face was left untouched.

### Added
- **A QR code for every lecture, for anyone.** Each lecture has its own QR code (*QR* on every lecture in Lectures, *QR code* in the bar of a live lecture), with a link that stays the same for that lecture, also when it is opened again: put it on a slide or a printed sheet before the lecture, or on the projector during it, where it now sits next to the six-digit code. Anyone can scan it with a phone camera: people who are signed in join at once; everyone else sees the lecture and taps *Join as guest*, with a name if they like, and needs no account or app. Scanned before the lecture starts, it joins the course, and the lecture opens on the phone when it starts. Download as PNG (slides) or SVG (print), print a sheet, copy the link, or make a new one if it got out (the old one stops working). The QR codes are made in Citrus itself (no library) and checked module for module against the reference encoder. Guests from a QR code belong to that lecture's university and have their own limit, so a whole lecture hall on one network can join (migration `026`).
- **Dates are picked in the Citrus look.** Every date and date-and-time field (scheduling lectures, the schedule, campus events) opens a calendar that matches the theme in light and dark: today ringed, the chosen day filled, *Today*, *Tomorrow* and *Next Monday*, common times as one tap, and full use by keyboard. On a phone it opens from the bottom like the other sheets. Forms get the same values as before.
- **Term overview for lecturers.** *Term overview* on the course page shows every finished lecture of the course: how well each one landed (a bar per lecture, coloured by how the class followed, with its flags), the trend over the last lectures, each topic of the course content from weakest to strongest, the hardest parts of the whole term (each opens the lecture's review at that exact part), and the students who chose to show their name, with only what they shared. *Ask Citrus about the term* in one tap; *For AI (.txt)* downloads it tagged, with instructions (`src/modules/live/term.js`, `GET /api/courses/:id/progress`).
- **My progress in a course, for students.** Their own results lecture by lecture next to the class, and every part they were unsure about, lost in or flagged, collected into one list: *Quiz me on all of these*, *Quiz me on this* (the quiz covers exactly those parts, from the key points taught there and the course files), *Explain it to me* and *My recap* at that part. Private to the student.
- **Ask Citrus reads the course files, any time.** A question that a course file answers gets the file's text and cites it as a source that opens the file, from any course the person teaches or takes, not only during lectures. *Ask Citrus* on a topic of the course content, or naming the course ("how am I doing in MA2?"), also brings in the term so far: the lecturer's overview, or the student's own progress. Course quizzes draw on the course files too.
- **File storage (Vercel Blob).** Connect a Blob store in Vercel and course files go straight from the browser to it (up to 100 MB each, no 4 MB limit), meeting recordings of any length are kept on the server, and the Android app's lecture audio is kept with the transcript and downloads as one file from the review. Files are private and only come back through Citrus after its access check; uploads use a short-lived pass for one exact path, size and type (`src/services/blob.js`, migration `025`). Without a store everything works as before.
- **Course content, like Moodle.** Each course page starts with its content: *General* for the whole course, then topics that open and close, with topics inside topics like folders. Lecturers add files (up to 4 MB, kept with their readable text), links, lectures and sub-topics from the **+** on any topic, arrange them from **⋯**, and can *go live*, *schedule a lecture* or *start a meeting* right inside a topic. Citrus reads the files of a lecture's topic (and the topics around it) during and after that lecture. Students open files and links and join or review lectures from the same outline (migration `024`, `src/modules/courses/sections.js`).
- **A review you can work with.** After a lecture the lecturer sees the numbers first, then a coloured chart: one bar per minute in green, amber and red, the parts of the lecture as bands coloured by how the class followed them, flags and struggles at their exact second. Tap a minute or a part for the exact moments: what was said, every change in the class, flags, and the answers of students who showed their name. *Citrus's quick take* gives the few points worth discussing in one click; then the notes and review as PDF, a conversation in the review, or *Continue in Ask Citrus*.
- **Students can show their name.** In the lecture or on their recap, a student can choose to show their name with their answers and flags (off unless they turn it on; migration `023`). The lecturer then sees what each of them struggled with and can make notes for them, ask them to talk after class (a notification), email them or ask Citrus about them. Everyone else stays anonymous, in the app and in every file.
- **Ask Citrus knows your lectures.** "What went wrong in my last lecture?" or the lecture's title is enough: the general assistant reads that lecture's record (the whole record for its lecturer, only their own for a student).
- **Two transcripts.** The plain transcript (.txt, what was said by time and part) and the lecture record for AI (.txt, everything tagged). Both start with instructions any AI follows.
- **The lecture record, tagged.** Everything that happens in a lecture in time order, each result tied to its exact second and the part of the lecture (P1, P2, ...): what was said, how the class followed after every answer, every minute, part and lecture average, flags, struggles, key points and gaps. `#follow-up` marks what the class did not understand; a student's own record marks `#target` (`src/modules/live/record.js`).
- **Every .txt explains itself.** The lecture record, the transcript, a student's own record, meeting minutes and the transcript kept on the device start with *Instructions for any AI*: what the tags mean and what to write, so ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Citrus turn it into the same notes without further explanation.
- **Notes as PDF, on the phone.** Citrus writes the lecture notes and a teaching review (every part that did not land gets an *Explain it better* box) or a student's own study notes, and the phone saves them as a formatted PDF: coloured boxes, tables with coloured status words, the minute-by-minute strip, graphs, Greek letters and maths symbols. Made on the device with no libraries (`core/pdf.js`); minutes and answers from the review save as PDF too. Nothing is stored on the server.
- **Review with Citrus.** In the lecture review the lecturer sees the lecture part by part with its average and talks it through with Citrus: why a part did not land, a better explanation, improved notes for next time. The conversation stays on the device.
- **Students' own results.** The recap shows a student's results part by part next to the class, the parts to work on first with *Explain this part to me*, and their own study notes. Never the lecturer's words verbatim or other students' answers.
- **Citrus for Android** (`android/`). Keeps recording a lecture or meeting with the screen off, in another app or after the app is closed: a recording service sends 30-second pieces to `POST /api/live/sessions/:id/audio` or `/api/workspaces/:id/audio`, which turn them into transcript lines at the times they were said and keep no audio. GitHub builds the APK (*Actions → Android app*). See [android/README.md](../android/README.md).

### Fixed
- **Citrus's voice no longer sounds rough on Windows laptops.** The voice arrives as 24 kHz audio in many small pieces, and a computer's audio runs at 44.1 or 48 kHz, so each piece was converted on its own and left a small click at every seam; together they made speech sound mumbled. The voice now plays on a 24 kHz audio context, so the pieces join exactly and the browser converts to the speakers once. Measured offline: 44.1 kHz pieces differed from the continuous audio by a click of up to 6 % of the signal, at 24 kHz by nothing. The live voice also starts each stretch of speech a little further ahead, so a slow network causes fewer stutters.
- **Large uploads work.** Every big course file or recording ended with "The upload was interrupted". The pass for a browser upload told the browser to send two headers that Vercel Blob does not allow from another website, so the browser refused to send the file at all (the settings those headers carried are already part of the pass itself). Checked against Vercel's own answer to the browser's pre-check, and the stand-in storage in the tests now refuses any header outside that list.

### Changed
- **A whole class can arrive at once.** The server used to wait on every database answer with its thread blocked, so students queued behind each other: fifteen guests arriving together took up to 23 seconds (the last one). Who is asking, guest sign-in, the first screen, scanning the QR code, joining, the student's live screen and answering now wait without blocking (`db.batchAsync`; the hosted-database client keeps each such query on a stream of its own, so it never disturbs another caller's transaction). Writes that arrive together are also committed together (Turso takes one write at a time, and a commit costs a few hundred milliseconds, so fifteen guests signing in took up to 25 seconds, one second each): guest sign-in is one transaction, and the database client sends everything written within a few milliseconds as one transaction. Measured with a stand-in database that commits one write at a time in 150 ms: one student takes 1.5 s, fifteen together 1.7 s (10 s without grouping). A database connection that has been closed now fails at once instead of waiting 30 seconds for an answer that cannot come.
- **A room of students arriving together stays quick.** One student scanning the QR code and answering cost 33 database round trips, and the server handles one at a time, so thirty students at once queued behind each other. Now 26 for the whole journey, and answers are cheaper still: the class is recounted for the lecturer once per 1.5 s however many students answer (the first answer shows at once), arriving in the room is one statement, guest sign-in is 4 calls instead of 6, signing in sends its two writes together, and the university's settings are kept for 10 seconds per server instance instead of being read on every visit (a change through the same instance shows at once). The lecturer's screen and the Puck follow a burst of answers about a second and a half later.
- **Pages load from the browser's cache.** Vercel served the versioned scripts and styles with "check again every time", so every page load asked about 40 files whether they had changed. Files with a version stamp are now cached for a year (`vercel.json`); files without one are still checked.
- **New server instances start quickly** and stop holding everyone up.** On Vercel a new instance starts often, and each one first made 11 database calls, 4 of them writes (the keys, which already existed), then 5 seconds later the hourly housekeeping as about 15 separate writes, during which it could answer nobody. Now an instance reads three times to start and writes nothing; housekeeping runs once an hour for all instances together (the last run is recorded) and sends its statements in one round trip.
- **Data survives deploys again.** The Turso database had been connected in Vercel with a new database branch for every deployment, so each deploy started the live site on an empty database. It is connected plainly now (checked: two deployments in a row use the same database), the health check shows when the database was set up (`databaseSince`), and the database client no longer gets stuck on a stream that failed ("stream not found" stopped the site from starting once while this was being fixed).
- **Pages read the database in one go.** Reads that do not depend on each other are sent together (`db.batch`, one round trip to the hosted database instead of one each): the course page went from 14 round trips to 3, the live lecture screens from 11 or 12 to 4, the start of every visit from 7 to 4. The lecturer's live screen no longer downloads the lecture notes on every refresh (only their names; a note is fetched when opened), the student's writes only when they first arrive, and a server instance no longer writes to the database when it starts.
- **Pages are much faster on the live site.** Every database query cost about 0.1 s, and pages ran many of them one after another. Two causes, both fixed: reads sent a foreign key setting first that the hosted database (Turso) handles slowly, which doubled the time of every read (about 100 ms down to about 50 ms; writes keep it, so foreign keys are still enforced); and the course page, its content, the term overview and *My progress* asked the database once or more for every lecture, so they got slower all term (a course with 30 lectures: about 120 queries down to about 20; the term overview about 8 per lecture down to 12 in total). The health check (`/api/v1/health`) now reports how long one database round trip takes (`databaseMs`).
- **Starting up after a deploy cannot leave the site down.** When several server instances start at once while one is updating the database, the first-run step no longer fails on a university that already exists, and an instance that could not start tries again a few seconds later instead of answering "Citrus could not start" for as long as it runs. (Seen once on 16 Nov 2026, right after the QR codes went live; the site recovered within seconds.)
- The Android app opens the live site (https://campusos-mdu.vercel.app) straight away instead of asking for the address.
- The inbox bell is centred again and swings when a notification arrives.
- **Recording never loses audio.** The screen stays awake while Citrus listens or records, and listening resumes by itself when the phone is unlocked (the record marks the gap). Meeting recordings are kept on the phone second by second; the server has no storage for audio, so a recording is saved to the phone and stays listed under *On this phone* until deleted.
- **"What is Citrus?" for presentations.** After you explain Citrus, say "Now you probably didn't understand that, did you?" or "In case that wasn't clear, hey Citrus, could you please elaborate further?" and Citrus explains itself with an excited face: the live classroom status (on screen and on the Citrus Puck), reviews for professors and recaps for students, NFC participation with the student ID, and information access on the side. The explanation is written in the app, so it works without the AI or a network; Ctrl/⌘+Shift+E or *Explain Citrus* in the command palette starts it without the microphone. Strict rules keep ordinary talk from setting it off (`features/pitch.js`, tests in `tests/pitch.test.js`).
- **Class status on the Citrus Puck.** Give the lecture a room and the class status goes to the pucks, door lights and displays registered there as the device command `class.pulse` (green, amber, red; counts only), so the lecturer sees when to explain again. The simulator and the Python client show it.
- **Full-screen notes with the island.** *Full screen* on the live lecture shows your PDFs, pictures and slides big, with a frosted toolbar at the bottom: previous, next and all notes, listening, ask Citrus by voice, chat with Citrus, the join code, exit. On phones the toolbar is a round button at the side that opens when tapped and can be dragged (`features/island.js`, `features/notes-viewer.js`).
- **Graphs in answers.** Ask for a graph and Citrus draws a real figure, like one in a textbook: functions, data, bars, pie charts, labelled axes, marked points and guide lines. Copy it as a picture or save it as PNG or SVG (`core/chart.js`; formulas are parsed, never run as code).
- **Meeting mode everywhere.** *Meetings* in the menu and a card on Home: start a meeting in one tap, rejoin a running one, open recent minutes. During a meeting, "Hey Citrus, explain …" is answered straight away and kept with the meeting's questions. Minutes are written by the main model with sections for decisions, action items, key details and questions raised, and can be turned into separate files by chat or voice: "one with the student questions and one without", "only the decisions" (migration `021`). Each set of minutes downloads as .txt.
- **Make a tool and More tools.** Describe a small tool ("a big clock for the projector") and Citrus makes it in the Citrus look, with four looks to choose from. It stays private unless you share it; shared tools are checked by an administrator, then anyone can add them from More tools. Tools run in a sandbox with no network, storage or access to the account (migration `022`, `src/modules/apps/`).

### Changed
- **Reopening a lecture works for students.** *Go live again* (course page, Lectures) starts a new live session for the lecture with the same join code and notes, and students on their recap see "live again, rejoin". Ending a lecture from the course page ends its live session too. Before, *Reopen* only changed the lecture's status, so there was nothing to join.
- **A calmer menu.** The menu shows the classroom loop: Lectures, Check-in, My progress, Meetings and Ask Citrus (lecturers: Lectures, Participation, Teaching, Meetings). The rest is under All tools, which now starts with *The classroom*, and stays reachable by search and voice. Home has a short "What Citrus does" card and no longer shows the lectures card twice.
- The text under the logo reads *Student project*.
- **Live lectures you can join with a code.** *Lectures → Go live* starts a lecture and shows a six-digit code, full screen for the projector. Students type it under *Join lecture* (or open `/join/<code>`), which also joins the course. The lecturer's screen has one bar with the time, the code, how many are in the room, listening on or off, and *End*, plus a short "show the code, then turn on listening" hint until the first student arrives. *Classroom* is now *Rooms & devices*, since it never started lectures.
- **Flag a moment.** During a lecture students mark the moment they need help with: didn't get this, too fast, need an example, or a question, with an optional note. The lecturer sees the flags live without names and can ask "What are they stuck on?". Each student can have Citrus explain their own flags from what was said at that minute (migration `018`).
- **Citrus uses the lecture transcript.** With *Keep the transcript* (on by default, chosen at the start) what Citrus hears is stored with the lecture. Students can ask Citrus about the lecture privately, during it and afterwards; the lecturer downloads it as a `.txt` with reading instructions, or deletes it. Without the option nothing is stored, as before.
- **Meetings with minutes.** A space's *Meeting* tab now runs a whole meeting: *Start meeting* turns on your microphone, every member can turn on theirs and what they say appears for everyone with their name, decisions, action items and questions are one tap, and *End meeting* drafts the minutes for every member. Ask Citrus "what did we decide?" mid-meeting. Audio recording stays optional (migration `019`).
- **Documents for Citrus.** Lecturers and administrators upload files as they have them (`/library`): PDFs, scanned PDFs, photos, screenshots, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, text. Text is taken from the file; pictures and scanned pages are read by the AI (Gemini, the lookup model), and so are PDFs whose fonts cannot be decoded, which go to the model as the PDF itself. Every page is filed in the knowledge library with its file and page; each document, or all of them, downloads as one `.txt` in the library format with reading rules at the top, which imports back unchanged (migration `020`, `ai.read`, `src/modules/library/`).
- **"Hit Citrus" wakes it.** The wake word now accepts the usual mishearings of "hey" ("hit", "hate", "high", "he") at the start of a phrase, and is stricter about false wakes: "let's hit campus tomorrow", "campus is closed" and "the campus library" never wake it, and a half-heard phrase waits for a whole word before deciding. Rules and tests in `features/wake-phrase.js`; the lecture microphone uses the same rules, stricter.
- **University knowledge.** The assistant now reads about 3,000 passages from the public OTH Amberg-Weiden website in German and English, with contact details as the pages print them, and the text of the published exam plans. See *What it knows about the university* in [AI.md](AI.md).
- **Academic snapshot.** Timetables, exam plans, study plans and semester dates come from the supplied snapshot `resources/CampusOS_OTH_AW_Academic_Data_2026-11-08.txt` (182 documents, 572 pages), searched by programme and semester and cited by page. It replaces the earlier extracted timetable and exam data.
- **Answers and pages in German.** Choose *Follow my language*, German or English for answers, in Settings or by asking. Any answer or page can be translated without changing the original. Sudden language switches and remarks like "can't do Deutsch, broken" are handled without fuss, also when speech recognition was listening for the other language.
- **Open links on command.** Answers use short link titles instead of long addresses, and "open it" opens the page or document in a new tab (university domains only).
- **German and English.** The interface, the assistant, voice commands and read-aloud work in both languages. The assistant understands sentences that mix them, misspellings and broken grammar. Voice input follows the language you speak; each language has its own voice.
- **Rename the assistant.** Each account can call it what it likes (Claudia, Max...); "Hey Claudia" and every label follow. This replaces the product-wide name decision in 1.1.0.
- **Settings by chat.** "Speak faster", "call yourself Nova", "stell auf Deutsch um", "turn off the animations". Changes show as a card and can be undone.
- **Campus map.** Both campuses, your position, and the way to your next lesson, with walking time and directions.

## 1.1.0 (November 2026)

### Added
- **Hosted database.** Set `TURSO_DATABASE_URL` and `TURSO_AUTH_TOKEN` and Citrus stores everything in Turso instead of `storage/campusos.db`, so it can run on Vercel (`src/db/remote.js`, no new dependencies). Keys that normally live in `storage/` move to the `app_keys` table (migration `005`), or come from `CAMPUS_MASTER_KEY` and `CAMPUS_NFC_KEY`. The server now listens when Vercel loads it. Recordings are unavailable without a disk.

### Changed
- **No `PUBLIC_URL`.** Citrus reads its address from the `VERCEL_*` variables Vercel sets, defaults to localhost elsewhere, and links follow each visitor's address. `TRUST_PROXY` and `COOKIE_SECURE` default on in Vercel. Behind your own proxy set `COOKIE_SECURE=1` and, for single sign-on, `OIDC_REDIRECT_URI`.
- **Name and brand.** The product is now **Citrus** and the assistant is **Citrus**. The new wordmark uses the edition's gradient ring as its O. The same ring is used for the favicon, the app icons (regenerated for both editions) and `/brand/mark.svg`.
- The assistant name is now part of the product, so the per-user and per-space name settings are gone. Older saved values are ignored.

### Added
- **Citrus's face.** The ring itself is an animated robot eye, with no pupil. It squashes flat to blink, slides and turns to look around, follows the pointer, follows the caret while you type and looks back up when you stop. Its shape shows how it feels: a gap circles the ring while it thinks, it turns into an upturned arc when an answer lands, pops open when stopped, droops when something fails, and closes when no model is connected. It shows on the assistant, home and settings screens, and as a static mark next to answers.
- **Animated explanations.** While Citrus explains something, its eye turns into a small animation: a formula, a counter, bars, a sorting pass, steps, stick figures, a cycle, a clock, code, an idea or a trend. The model asks for one with a one-line cue that the server strips and streams separately (`src/ai/visuals.js`, migration `004`). There is a cautious fallback when the model gives no cue. Illustrations can be replayed from the answer and turned off in Settings.
- **Full-screen chat.** Use the button in the header, Ctrl/⌘K → *Ask Citrus in full screen*, or `/assistant?full=1`. Esc leaves it.
- **"Hey Citrus" on every page.** Say "Hey Citrus, …" or just "Citrus, …". A microphone indicator in the top bar shows when it is listening and turns it off with one click. A switch in Settings → Assistant turns it on or off.
- **Other devices.** With `HOST=0.0.0.0` (the new default), the startup window and Admin → *Institution* list the addresses phones and laptops on the same Wi-Fi can open. Links follow the address each visitor used when `PUBLIC_URL` is empty. `share-online.bat` / `npm run share` starts a temporary HTTPS link through a Cloudflare quick tunnel.

### Fixed
- The assistant page no longer scrolls the whole window on desktop and mobile.
- The sidebar keeps the account menu visible on short screens. Only the menu scrolls.
- `npm run check` and `npm run backup` now read `.env`.

### Tests
- 51 automated tests, including the hosted-database driver against a protocol stand-in. `CAMPUS_TEST_DB=turso npm test` runs the whole suite that way.
- Visual cues under any stream chunking, the brand mark and manifest per edition, and migration 004.

## 1.0.0 (November 2026)

### Rebuilt
- **Back end restructured** into `src/` with a router, one module per feature, a composition root (`src/app.js`), numbered transactional migrations and central configuration. All existing API paths keep working, and 0.6 databases upgrade in place.
- **New front end** made of dependency-free ES modules: a safe `html``` template, router with page lifecycle, live updates over SSE, sheets and toasts, a command palette (Ctrl/⌘K). It replaces four stacked UI layers (`app.js`, `collaboration-ui.js`, `experience-ui.js`, `studio-ui.js`, `one/*`).
- **New design system:** frosted-glass surfaces over a soft aurora, edition accents, light, dark and system themes, a gradient home hero with circular quick actions, a floating mobile tab bar, and motion that respects reduced-motion settings.

### Added
- **Assistant:** conversation threads, streamed answers, Markdown, citations from the knowledge library, personal context, dictation, read-aloud, a custom assistant name and an optional hands-free wake phrase. Anthropic joins Ollama and OpenAI as a provider.
- **Knowledge library:** source links, categories, freshness labels, JSONL/Markdown/text import, one-click import of the bundled OTH public pack, and public search with sources.
- **Schedule:** course, personal and university-wide events, weekly repeats that are safe across daylight-saving changes, ICS import, a private subscription feed, and notifications to enrolled students.
- **Learning:** spaced review queue, practice sets (AI-written with validation, or the lecturer's own checks), per-course progress, and instant feedback on concept checks.
- **Spaces:** live captions with topic marking that feeds the linked course, a caption summary, a recorder with a live level meter, note deletion, leave and archive, and minutes printed without pop-ups.
